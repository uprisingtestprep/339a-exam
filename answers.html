Practice Test 1 Answer Key

The compressed gas pictogram shows a gas cylinder shape, unrelated to flammability.
  2. Limiting responsibility to only the supervisor or only the operator misrepresents how the system works. Limiting it to only the employer ignores the duties placed on individual workers under occupational health and safety law.

  4. The correct answer is A (Both arms extended and moved rapidly): the emergency stop signal uses both arms extended and moved rapidly, and it can be given by any worker at any time. The ordinary stop signal uses one flat arm moved slowly sideways, which is a different and less urgent signal. A motionless closed fist is not part of the standard signal set. A repeated downward thumb relates to boom down, not stopping.
  5. The correct answer is D (A safety data sheet): a safety data sheet, or SDS, explains a controlled product's hazards, safe handling, and first aid measures under WHMIS 2015. An inspection checklist covers equipment condition, not chemical hazards. A daily equipment log tracks operating hours and issues, not product hazards. A signed lift plan documents load and site details for a lift, unrelated to hazardous product information.
  6. The correct answer is D (Personal protective equipment): PPE sits at the bottom of the hierarchy of controls as the last line of defense, used after elimination, engineering, and administrative controls have been applied. Elimination removes the hazard entirely and ranks highest. Engineering controls physically separate workers from hazards and rank above administrative measures. Administrative controls, such as procedures and training, rank above PPE but below the first two categories.
  7. The correct answer is C (Pull the safety pin): the PASS method begins with pulling the pin to unlock the extinguisher's handle before it can be used. Squeezing the handle is the third step, not the first. Aiming at the base of the fire is the second step, done after pulling the pin. Sweeping side to side is the final step, performed only after the first three steps are complete.
  8. The correct answer is D (To isolate energy sources and prevent unexpected movement): lockout and tagout procedures isolate a crane's energy sources before maintenance so the machine cannot move unexpectedly and injure a worker. Recording operating hours is the purpose of a daily log, not lockout procedures. Confirming ground bearing capacity relates to site setup for lifting, not maintenance safety. Documenting a lift plan is unrelated to isolating energy during maintenance work.
  9. The correct answer is A (Operating radius and boom angle are both used together to locate rated capacity): mobile crane load charts are organized as a grid where the operator cross-references the radius row against the boom length column, and the angle is directly tied to the radius through the boom's geometry. Ground bearing pressure and outrigger spread affect where the crane can safely sit, not which capacity row applies. Hook block weight and parts of line matter for calculating net capacity after the chart value is found, not for locating the row itself. Wind speed and counterweight are separate factors applied after the base chart lookup, not the two variables that locate the rated capacity figure.
  10. The correct answer is D (This horizontal distance is called the operating radius of the lift): operating radius is the standard term for the horizontal distance from the center of rotation to the load, and it is the value load charts are built around. Boom length is the physical length of the boom itself, a separate measurement from the horizontal distance to the load. Tail swing radius describes how far the counterweight and rear of the upper structure swing, unrelated to load position. Boom angle is measured in degrees, not as a horizontal distance, so it cannot be the term being described here.
  11. The correct answer is C (GBP stands for ground bearing pressure under a loaded outrigger pad): GBP is the standard abbreviation riggers and operators use when calculating how much force per unit area an outrigger pad transmits to the ground. Gross boom pull is not a recognized industry term and has no standard abbreviation on a job site. General boom position is not a measurable planning value used in outrigger calculations. Gauge block pressure describes nothing in standard hydraulic terminology, making it an invented distractor with no real meaning.
  12. The correct answer is D (The hook block or headache ball weight must always be deducted first): the hook block hangs below the boom tip and its weight subtracts directly from the chart's gross rated capacity before any payload is considered, since the crane is already supporting it. Tire weight is part of the carrier's fixed structure already built into the chart rating and is never deducted separately. Counterweight is added to improve capacity, not subtracted from it. The upper cab structure is a fixed part of the machine's own rated weight and is not treated as a deductible rigging item.
  13. The correct answer is C (It accounts for the increased tension each sling leg carries at shallow angles): as a sling leg's angle from horizontal decreases, the same vertical load produces more tension in that leg, so the sling angle factor corrects the calculated capacity to reflect this real increase in force. Sheave diameter affects wire rope bending stress on the hook block, not sling leg loading. Engine horsepower has no bearing on rigging geometry or sling tension. Wire rope color coding is only used to identify rope diameter or grade, not to correct for angle.
  14. The correct answer is D (The tension in that sling leg increases sharply as the angle drops): as the angle from horizontal gets shallower, more of the leg's force works horizontally rather than vertically, so tension climbs sharply, especially below about 45 degrees. Tension decreasing is the opposite of what actually happens as the angle drops. Tension cannot reach zero while the leg is still supporting part of the load. Tension does not stay constant, since the geometry directly changes how much force each leg must carry to support the same vertical load.
  15. The correct answer is C (As operating radius increases across the chart, rated capacity steadily decreases): as the load moves farther from the crane's center of rotation, the overturning moment grows, so the chart's rated capacity drops steadily with increasing radius. Capacity increasing with radius is the reverse of how every mobile crane chart is actually built. Radius clearly does affect the chart, since it is one of the two main lookup variables. Boom angle and radius are directly linked through boom geometry, so capacity is never based on angle alone while ignoring radius.
  16. The correct answer is D (It is the single point where a load's entire weight is concentrated): the center of gravity is the balance point of a load, and rigging must be positioned relative to it so the load hangs level and stable during the lift. The boom tip's lowest reach point is a crane geometry measurement, not a property of the load itself. The midpoint between outrigger pads describes the crane's footprint, not the load being lifted. The hoist drum's geometric center is a machine component measurement with no connection to how a load's weight is distributed.
  17. The correct answer is A (Pads spread the outrigger point load over a larger area, lowering pressure): cribbing timbers or steel plates increase the contact area under each outrigger float, which reduces the ground bearing pressure and helps prevent the ground from failing under the load. Pads have no effect on the crane's rated lifting capacity, which comes from the load chart based on configuration. Pads do not change boom angle, which is set by the boom hoist system. Pads do not extend operating radius, which depends on boom length and angle, not outrigger footing.
  18. The correct answer is B (This limiting condition is properly known as structural capacity): structural capacity describes the point where the boom or crane structure itself would fail or bend under load, independent of whether the crane would tip over first. Tipping capacity is the opposite condition, governed by stability rather than structural strength. Dynamic capacity is not a standard load chart term used to describe a structural limiting condition. Static capacity is a general engineering term but does not specifically describe the structural failure limit referenced here.
  19. The correct answer is C (Structural capacity of the boom and machinery typically governs at short radii): at short radii the load is close to the crane, so tipping risk is low and the boom and structural components become the limiting factor instead. Tipping stability typically becomes the limiting factor only as radius increases and the overturning moment grows. Wind speed is a separate variable applied across the whole chart, not something that specifically governs at short radii. Parts of hoist line affect wire rope tension, not which limiting condition applies to the chart's rated capacity.
  20. The correct answer is D (Tipping stability of the crane typically governs at long operating radii): as the boom extends to reach farther out, the overturning moment increases and tipping becomes the limiting factor rather than structural strength. Structural strength of the boom is more likely to govern at short radii, not long ones, since bending stress is highest there. Wire rope diameter affects hoist line capacity, not the tipping-versus-structural relationship shown on the chart. Engine horsepower has no bearing on which stability limit applies at a given radius.
  21. The correct answer is C (It refers to the direction from which the load is being picked): operating quadrant describes which direction around the crane the load is being lifted from, such as over the front, over the side, or over the rear, since capacity differs by direction. Outrigger pads are physical equipment, not a load chart column heading. Boom sections describe the telescoping structure of the boom, unrelated to lift direction. Swing motor gears are internal mechanical components with no connection to how load charts label lift direction.
  22. The correct answer is A (Lifting over the side reduces stability since the fulcrum sits closer to the load): when lifting over the side, the tipping fulcrum line runs closer to the load's position than when lifting over the front, which reduces the crane's stability margin and lowers rated capacity. Boom length does not automatically need to increase just because the lift direction changes. Outrigger position does not have to be retracted for a side lift, and doing so would actually reduce stability further. The number of parts of line is chosen based on load weight, not the direction of the pick.
  23. The correct answer is A (Rated capacity must be reduced, and lifting may need to stop in high wind): wind exerts additional force on the boom and load, effectively increasing the overturning moment, so manufacturers require reduced capacity and eventually a full stop as wind speed rises. Wind cannot assist the boom hoist system, since it acts as an unpredictable side load rather than a helpful force. Capacity is affected by wind regardless of outrigger setup, since the load and boom are still exposed to wind force. Wind affects the crane at any boom position, not only when the boom happens to be fully retracted.
  24. The correct answer is D (It gains mechanical advantage and reduces the tension carried in each rope part): reeving multiple parts of line divides the load's weight across more rope segments, gaining mechanical advantage and lowering the tension each individual part must carry. Reeving does not change the maximum boom angle, which is a boom hoist system function. Reeving has no effect on ground bearing pressure, which depends on outrigger loading, not hoist rope configuration. Reeving does not extend operating radius, which is set by boom length and angle.
  25. The correct answer is A (Each part carries approximately one quarter of the total suspended load weight): with four parts of line and friction ignored, the total load is shared equally across all four rope segments, so each part carries about one quarter of the total weight. A single part carrying the full load would only be true with one part of line, not four. Half the load per part would only apply with two parts of line, not four. Tension is directly related to the number of parts, since more parts always means less tension per part for the same total load.
  26. The correct answer is B (Deduct jib weight whenever it is erected, and use the correct jib chart page): jib weight and geometry change the crane's moment balance, so the operator must deduct jib weight and reference the load chart page specifically printed for the jib-erected configuration. Ignoring the jib entirely would use an inaccurate capacity figure that does not reflect the added weight and reach. Adding jib weight on top of capacity is backward, since jib weight reduces available net capacity rather than adding to it. Switching only to the tipping portion of the chart ignores that structural limits can still apply and skips the required jib-specific chart page altogether.
  27. The correct answer is C (Rated capacity must exceed the total suspended weight or the lift is unsafe): the entire purpose of consulting a load chart is to confirm that the crane's rated capacity at the chosen configuration is greater than the actual total weight being lifted, including all rigging. Load weight does not determine which boom section gets telescoped first, since boom configuration is chosen based on the required radius and height. Load weight matters for every lift direction, not only lifts made over the rear. Load weight is precisely what determines which chart row and column apply, so claiming it has no bearing is incorrect.
  28. The correct answer is D (The boom hoist system, which changes boom angle, adjusts radius through luffing): the boom hoist system raises and lowers the boom, changing its angle and therefore the operating radius, which is the definition of luffing. The swing gear and slew ring rotate the upper structure horizontally and have no role in changing boom angle. The outrigger beam and jack system supports and levels the crane but does not move the boom. A load moment indicator display only reports information to the operator and does not physically adjust the boom's position.
  29. The correct answer is A (It continuously monitors radius, angle, and load to warn before capacity is exceeded): a load moment indicator tracks the crane's current radius, boom angle, and load in real time, comparing them against the chart to warn the operator before the rated capacity is exceeded. Calculating sling angle factors is a rigging planning task done before the lift, not a function of the LMI system. An LMI does not measure wind speed, which typically requires a separate anemometer. Simply recording parts of line reeved is not the core safety function of an LMI, which is focused on capacity monitoring.
  30. The crane's own counterweight is a fixed part of the machine's rated configuration and is not treated as a deductible load item. The carrier itself is part of the crane's base structure already accounted for in the chart rating, not a deduction. Outrigger pads support the crane's base and are not suspended below the hook, so their weight is not deducted from lifting capacity.
  31. Capacity does not increase with a smaller footprint, since a smaller footprint reduces stability rather than improving it. Capacity is not unchanged just because all four outriggers touch the ground, since the degree of extension still affects the stability footprint. Capacity clearly depends on outrigger spread as well as boom length, since both factors are printed on separate chart pages for different outrigger configurations.
  32. Wind direction is measured by separate instruments and has no connection to a boom angle indicator's reading. Hook block deflection from vertical is a rigging observation, not what a boom angle gauge measures. Outrigger beam positions are fixed once set up and are not related to the boom's angle reading during operation.
  33. Using sine of the angle would calculate the boom's vertical height component, not the horizontal radius. Dividing boom length by tangent of the angle does not correspond to the correct trigonometric relationship for this geometry. Claiming there is no defined relationship is incorrect, since radius, boom length, and angle are directly linked by basic trigonometry.

  71. The correct answer is C (Ground bearing pressure): Ground bearing pressure is the amount of force per square foot or square meter that the crane transmits into the soil beneath its supports. Mats and cribbing spread the load to lower this pressure.
  72.   73.
